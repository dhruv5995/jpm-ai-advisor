# Glossary

**Risk aversion score**: internal 1-5 scale used in client profiles, where 1
is capital-preservation-focused and 5 is growth/volatility-tolerant.

**Diversification**: spreading investments across assets whose returns don't
move in lockstep, to reduce the impact of any single position or market
segment underperforming.

**Drawdown**: the peak-to-trough decline in the value of a portfolio or asset
before it recovers to a new peak.

**Dollar-cost averaging**: investing a fixed amount at regular intervals
regardless of price, which reduces the risk of committing a lump sum at a
single bad entry point.

**Expense ratio**: the annual fee, as a percentage of assets, charged by a
fund to cover its operating costs.

**Rebalancing**: periodically buying/selling holdings to bring a portfolio
back to its target allocation as market moves cause it to drift.

**Tax-loss harvesting**: selling a losing position to realize a capital loss
that can offset taxable gains elsewhere, while staying invested in the asset
class.

**Time horizon**: the length of time before an investor expects to need to
draw on the invested funds; a primary driver of appropriate risk level.

**Sequence-of-returns risk**: the risk that poor investment returns early in
retirement (or early after a lump-sum withdrawal need) do outsized damage to
portfolio longevity, even if average returns over the full period are fine.
