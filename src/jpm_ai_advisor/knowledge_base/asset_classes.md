# Asset Class Reference

Illustrative long-run characteristics used for client education. Figures are
generic historical averages, not live market data or guarantees.

## US Large-Cap Equities
- Historical long-run nominal return: ~9-10% annualized
- Volatility (std. dev. of annual returns): ~15-17%
- Role in portfolio: primary growth engine; highest drawdown risk (can fall
  30-50% in a severe bear market)
- Time horizon fit: 7+ years

## US Bonds (Aggregate, Investment Grade)
- Historical long-run nominal return: ~4-5% annualized
- Volatility: ~4-6%
- Role in portfolio: capital preservation, income, ballast against equity
  drawdowns
- Time horizon fit: any, especially <5 years

## International Developed Equities
- Historical long-run nominal return: ~7-8% annualized
- Volatility: ~16-18%
- Role in portfolio: diversification away from US-only exposure

## Emerging Market Equities
- Historical long-run nominal return: ~8-10% annualized (highly variable by
  period)
- Volatility: ~20-24%
- Role in portfolio: higher growth potential, higher currency/political risk

## Cash & Cash Equivalents (money market, T-bills)
- Historical nominal return: tracks short-term rates (recent range ~4-5%)
- Volatility: near zero (nominal), but erodes real value under inflation
- Role in portfolio: emergency reserve, near-term spending needs, dry powder

## Real Estate (REITs)
- Historical long-run nominal return: ~8-9% annualized
- Volatility: ~18-20%
- Role in portfolio: income + inflation hedge, moderate correlation to
  equities
